<h2 id="introduction-to-decentralized-ai">Introduction to Decentralized AI</h2>







<p>Ever wondered what happens when AI meets the decentralized world? Brace yourself for an exhilarating journey!</p>







<p><strong>Distributed Artificial Intelligence</strong>&nbsp;(<strong>DAI</strong>) also called Decentralized Artificial Intelligences a subfield of&nbsp;<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence" target="_blank" rel="noopener">artificial intelligence</a>&nbsp;research dedicated to the development of distributed solutions for problems. DAI is closely related to and a predecessor of the field of&nbsp;<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_system" target="_blank" rel="noopener">multi-agent systems</a>.</p>







<p>Multi-agent systems and distributed problem solving are the two main DAI approaches. There are numerous applications and tools.</p>







<p>Decentralized AI refers to systems where artificial intelligence algorithms operate in a distributed manner, often on a blockchain or other decentralized networks. This approach offers benefits like enhanced security, transparency, and reduced reliance on central authorities. Here are some examples:</p>



<ol>
<li><strong>Decentralized Data Marketplaces</strong>: Platforms where individuals can securely sell or share their data directly with AI developers, without going through a central authority. This ensures data privacy and control while providing AI developers with a diverse data set.</li>



<li><strong>Blockchain-Based AI Models</strong>: AI models that are trained and operate on blockchain networks. Each node in the blockchain can contribute to the model&#8217;s training, making the process transparent and tamper-proof.</li>



<li><strong>Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs)</strong>: Organizations run by smart contracts on a blockchain, where AI algorithms can assist in decision-making processes based on predefined rules and member inputs.</li>



<li><strong>Peer-to-Peer AI Learning Networks</strong>: Networks where AI systems learn from each other in a peer-to-peer fashion, rather than relying on a central dataset or algorithm. This can lead to more robust and diverse AI learning outcomes.</li>



<li><strong>AI in Supply Chain Management</strong>: Using decentralized AI to track and verify supply chain operations. AI algorithms monitor and analyze transactions on a blockchain, ensuring transparency and efficiency in the supply chain.</li>
</ol>
